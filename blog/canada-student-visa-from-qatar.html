Canada Visa • Study Permit • Student Visa

If you are planning to study in Canada from Qatar in 2026, you will probably hear people use the term Canada student visa.

Technically, however, most international students coming to Canada for a program longer than six months need a study permit.

A study permit is the document that allows an eligible foreign national to study at a Designated Learning Institution, or DLI, in Canada. It is different from the visitor visa or Electronic Travel Authorization used to enter Canada. The current Canadian study permit fee is CAD 150.

For students applying from Qatar, the normal journey looks like this:

Choose an eligible Canadian school → receive a Letter of Acceptance → obtain a PAL/TAL if required → prepare financial and supporting documents → apply online → provide biometrics if required → receive a decision → travel to Canada

But several important Canadian student immigration rules have changed in recent years.

In 2026, students need to pay particular attention to:

  • Provincial or Territorial Attestation Letters
  • The international student cap
  • Higher proof-of-funds requirements
  • PGWP eligibility
  • Field-of-study rules for some programs
  • Restrictions on spousal open work permits
  • New school-transfer rules
  • The end of the Student Direct Stream
  • The 24-hour weekly off-campus work limit
  • Special rules for Qatari passport holders

This guide explains the Canada student visa process from Qatar in 2026 step by step.

Is a Canada Student Visa the Same as a Study Permit?

No.

A study permit gives you authorization to study in Canada.

A visitor visa, also called a Temporary Resident Visa or TRV, or an eTA is the travel document used to reach Canada, depending on your passport. Canada states clearly that a study permit itself is not a visa.

For a first study permit application, if Canada approves your permit and you need a TRV or eTA, IRCC generally issues the appropriate travel document as part of the approval process.

Special Rule for Qatari Citizens in 2026

There was an important Canada-Qatar immigration change in late 2025.

On November 25, 2025, Canada removed the visitor visa requirement for Qatari citizens. Qatari passport holders travelling to Canada by air are now generally visa-exempt and use an Electronic Travel Authorization, or eTA.

However:

Visa-free travel does not remove the study permit requirement.

If a Qatari citizen wants to study in Canada for more than six months, they still normally need a study permit.

When a visa-exempt applicant's initial study permit is approved, Canada generally issues the eTA automatically. The applicant does not normally need to submit a separate eTA application or pay the separate eTA fee for that approval.

What if You Live in Qatar but Hold Another Passport?

This distinction is extremely important.

Being a Qatar resident is not the same as being a Qatari citizen.

A person may live and work in Doha but hold a passport from:

  • India
  • Pakistan
  • Philippines
  • Bangladesh
  • Nepal
  • Sri Lanka
  • Egypt
  • Jordan
  • Lebanon
  • Kenya
  • Nigeria
  • Another country

Your Canadian travel-document requirements depend primarily on the passport you use, not simply on having a Qatar ID.

If you are not a citizen of Qatar, you should provide evidence of your lawful residence in Qatar when required. Canada's study permit application asks applicants applying from a country where they are not citizens to provide proof of their legal status there.

This can normally include evidence such as your valid Qatar residence status.

Do You Need a Study Permit for a Course Under Six Months?

Not always.

Canada generally does not require a study permit for a course or program lasting six months or less, provided the studies are not part of a longer program and can be completed during your authorized stay.

However, you still need the correct Canadian travel document.

For longer studies, a study permit is normally required.

If your main objective is a bachelor's degree, diploma, master's degree, PhD or another long-term program, you should expect to need a Canadian study permit.

Who Can Apply for a Canada Study Permit?

To study in Canada, you generally need to:

  • Be enrolled at a Designated Learning Institution
  • Prove you have sufficient money for tuition
  • Prove you can support yourself and accompanying family members
  • Show you can cover transportation costs
  • Meet Canada's criminal and security requirements
  • Complete a medical examination if required
  • Satisfy the immigration officer that you will respect your temporary immigration status

Receiving university admission does not automatically mean the Canadian government will approve your study permit.

The school admission decision and the immigration decision are separate.

Step 1: Choose a Designated Learning Institution in Canada

Before applying for a study permit, choose a Canadian institution that is authorized to receive international students.

This is called a Designated Learning Institution, or DLI.

IRCC maintains an official DLI list.

Universities, colleges and other institutions can have DLI status.

But there is another important issue:

Being a DLI does not automatically mean every program at that institution qualifies for a Post-Graduation Work Permit.

IRCC specifically warns students to check the PGWP status of both the institution and the individual program.

If working in Canada after graduation matters to you, check PGWP eligibility before paying your tuition deposit.

Step 2: Get a Letter of Acceptance

You normally need a valid Letter of Acceptance, or LOA, from your Canadian DLI before applying for the study permit.

The letter usually identifies information such as:

  • Your school
  • Program
  • Level of study
  • Study dates
  • Tuition
  • Student information

For post-secondary study permit applications, Canadian DLIs must verify the authenticity of Letters of Acceptance submitted to IRCC.

This system is designed to reduce fake admission letters and student fraud.

If the school fails to validate the LOA within IRCC's required timeframe, the study permit application can be returned and the processing fee refunded.

Therefore:

Never use a fake admission letter.

Confirm your acceptance directly with the Canadian institution.

Step 3: Get a PAL or TAL if Required

A major requirement for many international students is the:

Provincial Attestation Letter, or PAL

or

Territorial Attestation Letter, or TAL

The province or territory where you plan to study issues the PAL/TAL, usually through your educational institution. It confirms that a space has been allocated to you under Canada's international student system.

In many cases, the school may require you to:

  1. Accept the offer
  2. Pay part or all of the required tuition deposit
  3. Request or wait for the PAL/TAL

before you can submit the study permit application.

Important 2026 PAL/TAL Change for Master's and PhD Students

Canada introduced a significant exemption beginning January 1, 2026.

Students applying to a degree-granting master's or doctoral program at a public DLI generally no longer need to submit a PAL/TAL.

This can simplify the application process for qualifying graduate students.

Other PAL/TAL exemptions include certain:

  • Preschool students
  • Primary school students
  • Secondary school students
  • Exchange students
  • Students receiving certain Global Affairs Canada scholarships
  • Francophone Minority Communities Student Pilot participants
  • Other specified applicants

Do not assume you are exempt.

Check the exemption applying to your exact program.

How Long Is a 2026 PAL/TAL Valid?

A PAL/TAL issued for the 2026 study permit cap year is generally valid until December 31, 2026, unless an earlier expiry date appears on it.

A PAL/TAL from a previous cap year cannot simply be reused for a new 2026 application.

Also, if a study permit application was refused, a new PAL/TAL is generally required before reapplying when the PAL/TAL requirement applies.

Canada International Student Cap in 2026

Canada continues to operate an international student cap.

For 2026, the federal government stated that up to 180,000 study permits are expected to be issued to applicants who require a PAL/TAL, with allocations distributed among provinces and territories.

This makes early planning important.

Admission to a school does not necessarily mean a PAL/TAL allocation will automatically be available.

Studying in Quebec: You Need to Understand the CAQ

If you intend to study in Quebec, the process is different.

Most international students studying in Quebec need a:

Quebec Acceptance Certificate, or Certificat d'acceptation du Québec, commonly called a CAQ.

For Quebec applicants, the CAQ serves the relevant provincial attestation function, although separate Quebec rules apply.

You normally complete the Quebec immigration step before completing the federal study permit process.

Students considering Montreal or another Quebec destination should therefore not follow a generic Ontario, British Columbia or Alberta study permit checklist.

Step 4: Understand the 2026 Canada Student Visa Financial Requirement

Finances are one of the most important parts of a Canadian study permit application.

IRCC requires you to show that you have enough money, without depending on employment in Canada, to cover:

  1. Tuition
  2. Living expenses
  3. Transportation to and from Canada
  4. Costs for accompanying family members, where applicable

For programs lasting longer than one year, you need to show enough financial resources for your first year and explain how you plan to finance the remainder of the program.

What Can You Use as Proof of Funds?

IRCC lists several forms of financial evidence that may be used, depending on the case.

Examples include:

  • Proof of tuition payment
  • Proof of housing payment

  • Guaranteed Investment Certificate
  • Education loan

IRCC currently lists bank statements for the past four months among common examples of evidence.

You do not necessarily need every document on that list.

The goal is to demonstrate that the funds are real, available and sufficient.

Do You Need a GIC for a Canada Student Visa in 2026?

A GIC can still be used as one form of proof of financial support, but Canada no longer operates the old Student Direct Stream, or SDS.

The Student Direct Stream ended on November 8, 2024. Applications submitted after the closure are processed through the regular study permit process.

This means you should be careful with outdated websites that still describe SDS as an active 2026 pathway.

A GIC may still be useful evidence of funds, but it is not a separate SDS route.

Should You Include a Study Plan or Letter of Explanation?

A clear study plan can be useful where it helps explain your genuine educational purpose.

IRCC can refuse a study permit if the officer is not satisfied that your main purpose in Canada is studying or that you will leave Canada when required.

A useful explanation may address:

  • Why you selected Canada
  • Why you selected the institution
  • Why you selected the program
  • How the program relates to your education
  • How it relates to your professional history
  • Why the program makes sense for your future
  • How the studies are financed
  • Any educational gap
  • Any major career change
  • Your circumstances in Qatar
  • Your immigration history

Do not write a dramatic essay simply because someone told you that a long SOP guarantees approval.

A study explanation should be specific, credible and supported by the rest of the application.

Study Gap: Can You Still Get a Canada Student Visa?

A study gap does not automatically make a person ineligible for a study permit.

But if there is a significant gap between your previous education and proposed Canadian studies, explain what you were doing during that period.

Examples could include:

  • Employment
  • Professional training
  • Business activity
  • Family responsibilities
  • Additional education

The bigger issue is whether the proposed Canadian program makes sense when compared with your education, career history and stated goals.

Does Canada Require IELTS for a Study Permit?

There is no single universal IELTS score written into the basic federal study permit eligibility rules for every international student.

Your Canadian school may require English or French language evidence for admission.

Requirements vary by:

  • Institution
  • Program
  • Degree level
  • Language of instruction

Do not confuse a school's admission requirement with Canada's immigration requirements.

There is another reason language matters, however.

If you intend to apply for a Post-Graduation Work Permit after graduation, current PGWP rules impose language requirements on many graduates.

This should be considered before choosing a program, not after graduating.

Do You Need a Medical Exam?

Not every applicant follows exactly the same medical requirement.

For stays longer than six months, an immigration medical examination may be required if you lived or travelled for six consecutive months in certain countries during the year before entering Canada.

A medical may also be required for people intending to work in jobs where public health protection is important, including certain healthcare or child-related occupations.

If an Immigration Medical Examination is required, it must be completed by an IRCC-approved panel physician, not simply your normal doctor.

What Happens if Your Study Permit Is Approved?

If your application is approved from outside Canada, IRCC generally sends a port-of-entry letter of introduction.

This is not the study permit itself.

You present the required documents when travelling to Canada, and the study permit is normally issued when you enter Canada if you continue to meet the requirements.

Depending on your passport, you will also need the appropriate travel authorization.

For first-time study permit approvals, IRCC generally issues the required TRV or eTA automatically.

Keep important documents in your cabin luggage.

Approval of the study permit application does not mean you should travel without your supporting documents.

How Long Is a Canadian Study Permit Valid?

A study permit is normally valid for the length of the study program plus an additional 90 days.

The extra 90 days allow the student to prepare to leave Canada or apply to extend their stay if eligible.

If you complete your program earlier than expected, your study permit normally becomes invalid 90 days after your school first confirms that you completed the program, regardless of a later expiry date printed on the permit.

Can International Students Work in Canada in 2026?

Eligible international students may work off campus without a separate work permit if they meet IRCC's requirements.

These include being an eligible student at a DLI in an appropriate program and having the required study permit conditions.

During regular academic sessions, eligible students can currently work:

up to 24 hours per week off campus.

During eligible scheduled academic breaks, students can generally work unlimited hours off campus if they continue meeting the requirements.

Important

Do not use part-time work as your financial plan for obtaining the study permit.

IRCC requires you to demonstrate sufficient financial support without relying on employment in Canada.

New 2026 Co-op and Internship Rule

Another significant change took effect on April 1, 2026.

Post-secondary international students no longer generally need a separate co-op work permit for eligible student work placements such as co-op placements and internships.

Students must still meet the applicable conditions for the placement.

This is another area where older Canada student visa articles can now be outdated.

Can Your Spouse Work in Canada While You Study?

Not every international student's spouse can now obtain an open work permit.

Since January 21, 2025, spousal open work permit eligibility has been restricted.

A spouse or common-law partner may qualify if the student has a valid study permit and is enrolled in certain programs, including:

  • A master's degree of 16 months or longer
  • A doctoral program
  • Certain eligible professional degree programs
  • Certain specifically approved programs

Eligible professional degree programs include specified programs in fields such as:

  • Medicine
  • Dentistry
  • Law
  • Optometry
  • Pharmacy
  • Veterinary medicine
  • Nursing
  • Education
  • Engineering

Therefore, do not assume:

"If I get a student visa, my spouse automatically gets a work permit."

That is no longer correct.

Can Children Accompany an International Student?

Eligible spouses and dependent children can potentially accompany a student, but each family member must have the correct Canadian immigration status or travel authorization.

If family members travel with you, your financial-support requirement also increases.

For example, outside Quebec, the current annual living-fund requirement is:

  • One person: CAD 22,895
  • Two people: CAD 28,502
  • Three people: CAD 35,040

excluding tuition and transportation.

Minor children's schooling and study permit requirements depend on their circumstances.

Can You Get a Post-Graduation Work Permit After Studying in Canada?

Possibly.

A Post-Graduation Work Permit, or PGWP, can allow eligible graduates of qualifying Canadian programs to work in Canada after graduation.

But this requires careful planning.

Graduating from a DLI does not automatically make you PGWP eligible.

You need to check:

  1. Whether the institution is PGWP eligible
  2. Whether your specific program is eligible
  3. Program length
  4. Study format
  5. Language requirement
  6. Field-of-study requirement, where applicable
  7. Your full-time student status
  8. Other PGWP conditions

PGWP Requirements Students Should Know Before Choosing a Course

Under current rules, many PGWP applicants need language results.

For graduates of bachelor's, master's or doctoral degree programs, the current minimum is generally:

CLB 7 in English or NCLC 7 in French in all four language abilities.

For many college, polytechnic and other non-university programs, the language requirement is generally:

CLB 5 or NCLC 5 in all four abilities.

Bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates do not have a PGWP field-of-study restriction.

However, many students in other university, college, polytechnic or non-university programs who submitted their study permit application on or after November 1, 2024 must graduate in an eligible field of study to qualify for the PGWP.

For 2026, IRCC has frozen the eligible field-of-study list, meaning it will not add or remove eligible fields during 2026.

This is extremely important when selecting a diploma or certificate.

How Long Can a PGWP Be?

PGWP length depends on the type and duration of the program and the applicant's passport validity.

For an eligible master's degree program lasting at least eight months, a graduate can potentially receive a three-year PGWP even if the master's program itself is shorter than two years, provided all other requirements are met.

For many other programs:

  • Program between 8 months and under 2 years: PGWP may be issued for up to roughly the same length as the program
  • Program of 2 years or longer: PGWP may be issued for up to 3 years

Again, eligibility is not automatic.

Changing Your School in Canada Is More Complicated Now

Students should also know that Canada's school-transfer rules have tightened.

Students generally can no longer simply change post-secondary DLIs while continuing on the original study permit as older advice suggested.

A new study permit application may be required when changing DLIs.

If your study permit has already been approved while you are outside Canada and you decide to change your DLI before travelling, IRCC says you must submit a new study permit application with a new Letter of Acceptance and pay the applicable fees again.

Choose your school carefully before filing.

Common Canada Student Visa Refusal Reasons

IRCC states that a study permit application may be refused if an applicant does not:

  • Provide a valid Letter of Acceptance
  • Show sufficient financial support
  • Pass a required medical examination
  • Convince the officer that the main purpose of coming to Canada is studying
  • Convince the officer that they will leave Canada at the end of their authorized study period

Several practical problems can contribute to these concerns.

1. Weak Financial Evidence

Having the minimum balance alone is not always enough.

The source, availability and consistency of the money also matter. A large unexplained deposit immediately before applying may raise questions that clear financial documentation could have addressed.

2. Program Does Not Make Sense

For example, a senior professional with an advanced degree choosing an unrelated basic program without explanation may need to explain the academic and career logic.

3. Weak Study Purpose

A generic statement such as:

"Canada is a beautiful country with good education."

does not explain why this particular applicant needs this particular Canadian program.

4. Inconsistent Information

Your:

  • Education
  • Employment
  • Finances
  • Family history
  • Study plan
  • Application forms

should not contradict each other.

5. Insufficient Evidence of Temporary Compliance

Canada requires temporary residents to comply with the conditions of their authorized stay. A person may have long-term career ambitions while still being required to respect the conditions of the study permit.

6. Fake or Misleading Documents

Do not use:

  • Fake bank statements
  • Fake employment letters
  • Fake admission letters
  • Fake tuition receipts
  • False education records

IRCC's LOA verification system is specifically designed to allow DLIs to confirm whether post-secondary Letters of Acceptance are genuine.

No consultant can legitimately guarantee study permit approval.

Final Thoughts: Applying for a Canada Student Visa From Qatar in 2026

The Canadian study permit system has become more structured.

Students applying from Qatar in 2026 should not begin with:

"Which agent can get me a visa?"

Start with:

  • Which school am I attending?
  • Is it a DLI?
  • Is my program PGWP eligible?
  • Do I need a PAL/TAL?
  • Can I genuinely afford tuition and living expenses?
  • Does this program make sense for my education and career?
  • What will my immigration options actually look like after graduation?

The major 2026 rules to remember are:

  1. A study permit, not simply a "student visa," is the main authorization for long-term study in Canada.
  2. Many post-secondary applicants require a PAL/TAL, but qualifying master's and doctoral students at public DLIs are exempt beginning January 1, 2026.
  3. A single student outside Quebec currently needs at least CAD 22,895 in first-year living expenses, excluding tuition and transportation.
  4. The Student Direct Stream is closed.
  5. Eligible students can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic sessions.
  6. PGWP eligibility now requires much more careful program selection, especially for non-degree programs.
  7. Qatari citizens are now generally visa-exempt for air travel, but they still require a study permit for long-term study.

The safest strategy is to select the right Canadian institution and program before paying major fees or filing the immigration application.

Canadian student immigration rules continue to change, so always verify the current requirements with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada before submitting your application.
