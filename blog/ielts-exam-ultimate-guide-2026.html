What Is IELTS?

IELTS stands for International English Language Testing System.

It assesses your ability to communicate in English across four language skills:

  1. Listening
  2. Reading
  3. Writing
  4. Speaking

IELTS uses a 9-band scoring system, with separate scores for each skill and an overall band score.

IELTS is jointly owned by:

  • British Council
  • IDP IELTS
  • Cambridge University Press & Assessment

The test is used for purposes including higher education, migration, employment and professional registration.

IELTS 2026: What Has Changed?

One of the largest IELTS changes in 2026 concerns how the exam is delivered.

IELTS announced on March 5, 2026 that traditional paper-based IELTS would stop being offered from mid-2026, with exact implementation dates varying by market. IELTS on the computer has become the standard delivery method.

For people who prefer handwriting, a Writing on Paper option is being introduced in selected markets.

Under this format:

  • Listening is completed on computer
  • Reading is completed on computer
  • Writing is handwritten on paper
  • Speaking remains with an IELTS examiner

The scoring standard, skills tested and test construct do not change simply because the delivery method changes.

In Qatar, the British Council currently lists:

  • IELTS on Computer
  • IELTS on Computer with Writing on Paper
  • IELTS Academic Online
  • IELTS for UKVI

among its current options.

Types of IELTS Tests in 2026

Choosing the correct test is the first major decision.

The two main IELTS versions are:

IELTS Academic

Choose IELTS Academic if you intend to:

  • Apply for undergraduate study
  • Apply for postgraduate study
  • Attend a university or higher education institution
  • Complete certain professional registrations

IELTS states that Academic is designed for university-level study and professional registration.

IELTS General Training

Choose IELTS General Training when it is required for purposes such as:

  • Migration
  • Employment
  • Training
  • Study below degree level

IELTS specifically identifies General Training as a migration-oriented test for countries including Canada, Australia, New Zealand and the UK.

Important for Canada

If you are using IELTS for Canada Express Entry, IRCC requires:

IELTS General Training.

IELTS Academic cannot be substituted for Express Entry simply because your Academic band score is high. IRCC also states that it does not accept IELTS One Skill Retake for Express Entry.

IELTS Academic vs General Training

The two tests are similar, but not identical.

Section IELTS Academic IELTS General Training
ListeningSameSame
ReadingAcademic textsEveryday/work-related texts
Writing Task 1Graph, chart, table, diagram or processLetter
Writing Task 2EssayEssay
SpeakingSameSame

IELTS confirms that Listening and Speaking are the same in both tests, while Reading and Writing differ.

Do not book Academic simply because you think it sounds more prestigious.

Book the version required by the organization or immigration program receiving your score.

What Is IELTS for UKVI?

IELTS for UKVI is a Secure English Language Test approved for relevant UK visa and immigration purposes.

Depending on the UK immigration category, an applicant may need:

  • IELTS for UKVI Academic
  • IELTS for UKVI General Training
  • IELTS for UKVI Life Skills

The exact score and test type depend on the UK immigration route.

If your goal is Canada rather than the UK, do not automatically pay extra for IELTS for UKVI.

Use the test required by Canada.

IELTS Test Format in 2026

The IELTS test contains four sections:

Listening → Reading → Writing → Speaking

The total test time is approximately 2 hours and 45 minutes.

IELTS Listening Test

The IELTS Listening section contains 40 questions.

Each correct answer receives one raw mark, and the raw score is converted into an IELTS band score.

You hear different types of recordings and answer questions while listening.

Common question formats can include:

  • Multiple choice
  • Matching
  • Form completion
  • Note completion
  • Table completion
  • Flow-chart completion
  • Sentence completion
  • Short answers
  • Map or diagram labelling

How to Improve IELTS Listening

Do not prepare only by watching English movies.

IELTS Listening tests specific examination skills.

Practice:

  • Understanding different accents
  • Predicting answer type
  • Recognizing synonyms
  • Following directions
  • Writing numbers correctly
  • Recognizing dates
  • Identifying names
  • Spelling
  • Listening while reading questions
  • Moving quickly when one answer is missed

One common mistake is continuing to think about a missed question while the recording moves to the next one.

If you miss an answer, continue.

IELTS Reading Test

IELTS Reading also contains 40 questions.

The Academic and General Training versions use different types of reading material, even though both are scored using the IELTS 9-band system.

Common question types include:

  • Multiple choice
  • True/False/Not Given
  • Yes/No/Not Given
  • Matching headings
  • Matching information
  • Matching features
  • Sentence completion
  • Summary completion
  • Table completion
  • Diagram completion
  • Short-answer questions

IELTS Reading: True, False and Not Given Explained

This question type causes many unnecessary mistakes.

TRUE — The statement agrees with the information in the text.

FALSE — The text directly contradicts the statement.

NOT GIVEN — The passage does not provide enough information to decide.

Do not use your personal knowledge.

IELTS Reading is testing what the passage says, not what you know about the topic.

IELTS Writing Test

IELTS Writing lasts 60 minutes and contains two tasks.

Task 2 is worth twice as much as Task 1 when calculating your Writing score.

This means poor time management can be expensive.

A sensible target is approximately:

  • Task 1: 20 minutes
  • Task 2: 40 minutes

IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

In Academic Task 1, you may need to describe:

  • Bar chart
  • Line graph
  • Pie chart
  • Table
  • Process
  • Diagram
  • Map
  • Combination of visual information

You need to write at least 150 words.

You are not normally being asked to give your personal opinion.

You need to identify and communicate the major information clearly.

A strong Academic Task 1 usually needs:

  1. Introduction
  2. Overview
  3. Key details
  4. Comparisons where relevant

The overview is particularly important.

Do not describe every number individually.

Identify the biggest patterns.

IELTS General Training Writing Task 1

General Training Task 1 requires you to write a letter.

The situation may require a:

  • Formal letter
  • Semi-formal letter
  • Informal letter

You need at least 150 words.

Your response should address all parts of the task and use an appropriate tone.

For example, a complaint to a company should not sound like a message to your best friend.

IELTS Writing Task 2

Task 2 is an essay for both Academic and General Training.

You need to write at least 250 words. IELTS recommends spending about 40 minutes on this task because it contributes twice as much as Task 1 to the final Writing score.

Typical essay tasks can ask you to:

  • Give an opinion
  • Discuss two views
  • Discuss advantages and disadvantages
  • Explain causes and solutions
  • Answer two questions
  • Evaluate an argument or issue

Do not memorize a single essay and force it onto every topic.

Examiners assess whether you actually answer the question.

How IELTS Writing Is Scored

Writing is evaluated using four criteria:

  1. Task Achievement / Task Response
  2. Coherence and Cohesion
  3. Lexical Resource
  4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

Task Response — Did you answer the actual question? A grammatically beautiful essay that avoids the question can still score poorly.

Coherence and Cohesion — Are the ideas logically organized and connected? Linking words should support the logic rather than replace it.

Lexical Resource — Using a simpler word correctly is better than using an advanced word incorrectly.

Grammatical Range and Accuracy — A useful goal is controlled variety: simple sentences, compound sentences, complex sentences, relative clauses, conditionals, appropriate passive structures. Accuracy still matters.

IELTS Speaking Test

The IELTS Speaking exam takes approximately 11 to 14 minutes.

It is an interview between the candidate and an IELTS examiner and contains three parts.

Speaking Part 1

Approximately 4 to 5 minutes.

You answer familiar questions about subjects such as:

  • Home
  • Family
  • Work
  • Studies
  • Hobbies
  • Daily life

Do not answer every question with one word.

But do not turn a simple question into a five-minute speech either.

Speaking Part 2

You receive a task card.

You have one minute to prepare, then speak for up to approximately two minutes.

Use the preparation minute to write keywords, not complete sentences.

A useful mental structure is:

What → When → Where → Who → Why → How you felt

This helps keep you speaking when ideas run out.

Speaking Part 3

Part 3 develops the Part 2 theme into broader and more abstract questions.

It normally lasts around 4 to 5 minutes.

This part often requires:

  • Explaining reasons
  • Comparing ideas
  • Predicting
  • Evaluating
  • Discussing social issues
  • Giving examples

How IELTS Speaking Is Scored

Your Speaking score is based on four equally weighted criteria:

  1. Fluency and coherence
  2. Lexical resource
  3. Grammatical range and accuracy
  4. Pronunciation

Do You Need a British Accent?

No.

The key issue is whether your pronunciation can be understood clearly.

IELTS specifically evaluates pronunciation based on how understandable your speech is.

Do not waste preparation time trying to imitate an artificial accent.

How IELTS Band Scores Work

IELTS uses band scores from 0 to 9.

Common interpretations include:

Band General Skill Description
9Expert
8Very good
7Good
6Competent
5Modest
4Limited

You receive separate scores for:

  • Listening
  • Reading
  • Writing
  • Speaking

You also receive an overall band.

How Is the Overall IELTS Band Calculated?

The overall score is calculated from the average of your four skill scores and rounded according to IELTS rules.

Example:

  • Listening: 8.0
  • Reading: 7.0
  • Writing: 6.5
  • Speaking: 7.5

Total: 29. Divide by four: 7.25. The overall IELTS score becomes: 7.5

IELTS states that an average ending in .25 is rounded to the next half band, while an average ending in .75 is rounded to the next whole band.

Is There a Pass or Fail in IELTS?

There is no one universal IELTS "pass mark."

The score you need depends on the organization receiving the result.

For example:

  • One university may ask for 6.0
  • Another may require 6.5
  • A professional regulator may require 7.0 in every skill
  • An immigration program may use its own language threshold

Your target should be: "I need these exact scores for this exact purpose."

IELTS for Canada Immigration in 2026

If your goal is Canada permanent residence through Express Entry, you need to be particularly careful.

IRCC accepts IELTS for Express Entry only in the:

General Training format.

IRCC explicitly states that IELTS One Skill Retake is not accepted for Express Entry.

IELTS Score Required for Canada Express Entry

Canada converts IELTS results into Canadian Language Benchmark, or CLB, levels.

For the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the minimum first-language level is CLB 7 in all four abilities.

CLB 9 is an important target for many Express Entry applicants because higher language results can improve immigration scoring.

For IELTS General Training, IRCC currently maps CLB 9 to:

IELTS for Canada Study Permit

Students usually encounter IELTS first as part of school admission, not because every Canadian study permit applicant is subject to one universal IELTS band requirement.

Canadian institutions set their own admission standards.

A university might require:

  • Overall 6.5
  • No section below 6.0

while another program may set a different threshold.

Always check the institution and program directly.

IELTS Academic Online

IELTS Academic can also be taken remotely online in locations where the service is available.

IELTS Online is Academic only. The Listening, Reading and Writing sections are completed online, while Speaking remains a live interaction with an examiner through video. Results are generally available in around 6 to 8 days.

Before choosing IELTS Online, verify that the university, regulator or other organization you are applying to accepts that delivery format.

It is not an alternative to IELTS General Training for Canadian Express Entry.

IELTS One Skill Retake in 2026

IELTS One Skill Retake, or OSR, allows an eligible candidate to retake only one section: Listening, Reading, Writing or Speaking, instead of repeating the entire exam.

To qualify, you generally need to:

  • Have taken a full IELTS on computer test at a participating centre
  • Take the One Skill Retake within 60 days
  • Retake only one skill
  • Use One Skill Retake only once per original full test

IELTS states that OSR results are normally available within 3 to 5 days.

Important for Canada Express Entry

Do not use IELTS One Skill Retake for Express Entry.

IRCC explicitly states that it does not accept OSR for Express Entry.

How Long Is IELTS Valid?

IELTS recommends that test results generally be considered valid for two years after the test date.

For Canada Express Entry, IRCC is stricter. Your language test must be less than two years old both when you complete your Express Entry profile and when you submit your permanent residence application.

How Long Should You Prepare for IELTS?

There is no single correct preparation period.

A useful preparation process is:

Step 1: Take a Diagnostic Test — Determine your current level in Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking under timed conditions.

Step 2: Identify the Required Score — For Canada Express Entry CLB 9: L8 / R7 / W7 / S7.

Step 3: Identify Your Weakest Skill — Do not divide preparation time equally if one skill is significantly weaker.

Step 4: Learn the Test — Question types, timing, instructions, marking criteria, word limits, computer interface.

Step 5: Practice Under Time Pressure — Eventually you need full sections completed under exam conditions.

A Practical 8-Week IELTS Preparation Plan

Weeks 1 and 2: Understand and Diagnose — Learn every section, complete an initial mock test, identify weak areas, learn question types, understand band descriptors.

Weeks 3 and 4: Build Skill — Vocabulary in context, grammar errors, reading speed, listening accuracy, essay structure, speaking fluency.

Weeks 5 and 6: Target Weaknesses — Spend more time on the lowest-scoring component. For Writing: get feedback, practice introductions and conclusions. For Speaking: record yourself, reduce pauses.

Week 7: Full Mock Tests — Practice entire sections under realistic timing.

Week 8: Stabilize — Focus on accuracy, timing, sleep, familiar question types, avoiding repeated mistakes.

IELTS Writing Mistakes That Keep Candidates Below Band 7

  • Memorized Essays — If the question changes slightly, a memorized essay often becomes irrelevant.
  • Too Many "Advanced" Words — Incorrect vocabulary lowers clarity.
  • No Clear Position — In an opinion essay, the examiner should understand your position.
  • Weak Paragraph Development — Main idea, explanation, example or development.
  • Ignoring Task 1 — Task 1 still contributes to your Writing score.
  • Spending Too Long on Task 1 — IELTS recommends roughly 20 minutes for Task 1 and 40 minutes for Task 2.

IELTS Speaking Mistakes to Avoid

  • Memorizing full answers
  • Giving one-word answers
  • Trying to speak too fast
  • Forcing idioms
  • Faking an accent

IELTS Reading Mistakes to Avoid

  • Reading every passage slowly from beginning to end
  • Ignoring keywords and synonyms
  • Spending too long on one question
  • Confusing False with Not Given
  • Adding personal knowledge
  • Ignoring word limits in answers
  • Misspelling answers

IELTS Listening Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not reading questions before the recording
  • Missing plurals
  • Incorrect spelling
  • Ignoring word limits
  • Writing the first number heard even after the speaker corrects it
  • Becoming distracted after missing an answer

Computer IELTS vs Writing on Paper

In Qatar, IELTS candidates can currently choose IELTS on computer, and the British Council also lists a Writing on Paper option.

IELTS on Computer May Suit You If:

  • You type quickly
  • Your handwriting is weak
  • You edit writing frequently
  • You prefer digital reading
  • You want faster results

Writing on Paper May Suit You If:

  • You think more clearly when handwriting
  • You dislike typing essays
  • Your handwriting is clear and fast
  • You are comfortable reading Listening/Reading questions on screen

Final Thoughts: IELTS Exam in 2026

IELTS is not simply a test of whether your English is "good" or "bad."

It measures four different abilities: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking.

And the score you need depends entirely on what you are trying to achieve.

For students, that may mean IELTS Academic.

For Canada Express Entry candidates, that means IELTS General Training.

For someone applying under a UK route, it may mean IELTS for UKVI.

The biggest practical lessons for 2026 are:

  1. Choose the correct IELTS test before booking.
  2. Computer-based IELTS is now becoming the standard global delivery method.
  3. Qatar currently offers computer IELTS and a Writing on Paper option through British Council.
  4. IELTS General Training is required for Canada Express Entry.
  5. One Skill Retake is not accepted for Express Entry.
  6. IELTS results are generally treated as valid for two years.
  7. For Canadian Express Entry, CLB 9 corresponds to IELTS General Training scores of L8, R7, W7 and S7.

Do not prepare only to achieve a high overall number. First find the exact score required for your university, profession or immigration program, then build your preparation around each individual skill.
